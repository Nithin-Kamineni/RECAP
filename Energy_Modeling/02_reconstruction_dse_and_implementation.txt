12. THE FANOUT/REUSE TRADEOFF IS THE CENTRAL DSE
======================================================================

Treat reconstruction placement as a problem involving both temporal reuse
and spatial fanout.


12.1 AXIS 1: TEMPORAL REUSE

Question:
How many times is a reconstructed value consumed after reconstruction?

Low temporal reuse                         High temporal reuse
        |                                          |
        +------------------------------------------>
           Reconstruction cost becomes easier
           to amortize when the full result is retained

High temporal reuse favors:

Reconstruct once
      |
      v
Full decoded register
      |
      v
Reuse repeatedly

Relevant examples:
- Weight-stationary architectures.
- Simba.
- Portions of Eyeriss RS and RS+ execution.

Important distinction:
High reuse helps only when reconstruction is outside the repeated-use
loop or its result is retained. Reconstructing on every use does not gain
this amortization.


12.2 AXIS 2: SPATIAL FANOUT

Question:
How many PEs consume the same weight?

                   Source
                     |
                     v
               Reduced value
                     |
           +---------+---------+
           v         v         v
          PE        PE        PE

Two basic choices:


PRE-FANOUT RECONSTRUCTION

           Encoder
              |
              v
         Full 8-bit value
           /  |  \
          v   v   v
         PE  PE  PE

Characteristics:
- One encoder at this fanout source.
- One reconstruction can serve several consumers.
- Downstream multicast carries full-width values.


POST-FANOUT RECONSTRUCTION

        Reduced 4-bit value
           /  |  \
          /   |   \
         v    v    v
        XOR  XOR  XOR
         |    |    |
         v    v    v
        PE   PE   PE

Characteristics:
- Multiple destination encoders.
- Reduced-width network transport.
- Reconstruction may occur separately at each destination.
- Replicated area and leakage may matter.

This tradeoff requires an explicit experiment.

A hierarchical network also permits intermediate placement:
- Before global fanout.
- At destination-cluster boundaries.
- After local fanout.
- At individual PE inputs.

13. EXAMPLE SHOWING WHY THE OPTIMUM MOVES
======================================================================

Illustrative representation:
- 8 total bits.
- 4 independent bits.
- 4 reconstructable bits.
- 64 uses of a weight.

This assumes independent reconstruction is feasible at the stated
granularity. See section 15 for group dependencies.


OPTION A: RECONSTRUCT BEFORE PE RF

NoC:             4-bit representation
Encoder:         1 reconstruction
RF storage:      8-bit weight
RF reads:        64 x 8-bit reads


OPTION B: RECONSTRUCT AFTER PE RF

NoC:             4-bit representation
RF storage:      4-bit representation
RF reads:        64 x 4-bit reads
Encoder:         64 reconstructions

Under the simplified read-and-encoder comparison, B beats A when:

64 * [E_RF(8) - E_RF(4)] > 63 * E_encoder

Interpretation:
- B saves RF read energy on 64 reads.
- B performs 63 additional reconstructions compared with A.

This equation omits differences in writes, capacity/leakage, controls,
timing, packing, and any other unequal costs. Include those in the full
model.


OPTION C: REDUCED RF PLUS DECODED REGISTER

NoC:                      4-bit representation
RF storage:               4-bit representation
RF reads:                 1 x 4-bit read
Encoder:                  1 reconstruction
Decoded-register reuse:   64 uses

Option C can potentially outperform both A and B.

Research implication:
Include reconstruction-result retention in the DSE rather than comparing
only fixed pipeline boundaries.

IMPLEMENTATION CLARIFICATION:
A fair baseline may already retain a full weight in an operand register,
avoiding 64 RF reads. Compare equivalent reuse mechanisms. Do not attribute
ordinary register-reuse benefits entirely to ECC reconstruction.

For nonconsecutive reuse, a single register may be insufficient. Evaluate
the actual schedule and, if appropriate, a small cache with its lookup,
replacement, fill, storage, and energy costs.

14. EYERISS V2 ALREADY USES COMPRESSION
======================================================================

Eyeriss v2 processes sparse weights and activations using compressed CSC
representations, with separate weight address and data scratchpads.

ECC reduction therefore operates within an already compressed datapath.

Baseline representation:

CSC metadata
     +
Full weight-value bits

Proposed representation:

CSC metadata
     +
ECC-independent weight bits

Reducing weight values from 8 bits to 4 bits does not halve total storage
when entries also contain:
- Count bits.
- Address metadata.
- Column metadata.

Effective compression ratio:

C_effective =
    (Original value bits + Metadata bits)
    /
    (Reduced value bits + Metadata bits)

This expression assumes unchanged metadata. If the proposed organization
changes metadata or introduces group identifiers, include those costs.

Report value-bit reduction separately from complete representation
reduction.

Do not replace a 12-bit compressed entry with a 4-bit entry merely because
an 8-bit weight value was reduced to 4 bits; determine what every stored
field represents.

15. XOR RECONSTRUCTION MAY REQUIRE A COMPLETE ECC GROUP
======================================================================

The encoder may operate at codeword granularity rather than independent
weight granularity.

Illustrative parity relationships:

p0 = m0 XOR m1 XOR m4
p1 = m1 XOR m2 XOR m5

If these message bits belong to different weights, reconstructing one
weight can require retained bits from several weights.

The hardware may not support:

4 retained bits of Weight 1 --> Encoder --> Full Weight 1

It may instead require:

                 Retained bits W1
                        |
Retained bits W2 --------+------+
                        |      |
Retained bits W3 --------+------+
                        |      |
                        v      v
                      ECC encoder
                           |
                           v
                  Reconstructed group
                           |
                           v
                     W1 W2 W3 ...

Consequences:
- Group-assembly buffering.
- Encoder input width.
- Reconstruction latency.
- Packing and unpacking.
- Routing.
- Codeword alignment.
- Scheduling.
- Whether weights can be reconstructed independently.
- Whether all dependencies are available at a chosen boundary.

Define reconstruction granularity:

G_rec =
    Number of weights whose retained information must be available
    simultaneously to reconstruct an ECC group.

The exact dependency set can be more informative than a single scalar.

Immediate implementation task:
Inspect the actual ECC generator relationships and weight-to-codeword
mapping before designing a PE-local encoder.

Determine:
- Which retained bits are needed for each reconstructed output bit?
- Which weights supply those bits?
- Whether the dependencies reach the same PE or cluster together.
- Whether sparse compression removes or reorders needed values.
- Whether group assembly requires additional transfers or storage.

Reconstruction granularity may be as important as XOR count.

16. WHAT SRAM READ/WRITE SAVING REALLY MEANS
======================================================================

Avoid unsupported claims such as:

"Reducing weights from 8 bits to 4 bits reduces SRAM energy by 50%."

A 64-bit SRAM access can still activate:
- Decoder.
- Wordline.
- Bitlines.
- Sense amplifiers.
- Peripheral circuitry.

Storing a representation such as:

xxxx0000 xxxx0000 ...

inside full-width words does not automatically halve physical energy.

Exploit the representation physically.


BASELINE PACKING

64-bit SRAM word
    =
8 full weights x 8 bits


REDUCED PACKING

64-bit SRAM word
    =
16 reduced weights x 4 bits

Now one access can return twice as many reduced weight representations.

Potential benefit:
Fewer physical accesses for the same number of useful weights.

Alternative:
A physically narrower memory organization, if supported by the chosen
memory implementation.

Model:
- Physical word width.
- Number of entries.
- Banking.
- Access granularity.
- Packing efficiency.
- Padding/alignment.
- Metadata.
- Packing/unpacking overhead.
- Group-assembly requirements.
- Number of useful values delivered per access.

Reduced logical bit count and reduced physical energy are related but are
not interchangeable.

Define reconstruction amortization factor:

A_rec =
    N_MAC_weight
    /
    N_reconstruct

Interpretation:
Number of useful weight-consuming MAC operations supported per
reconstruction.

A larger value generally indicates better amortization.

Use consistent granularity:
If one reconstruction produces a group of weights, document that the
denominator counts group reconstructions.

Additional implementation accounting needed where applicable:
- Metadata traffic.
- Group-assembly reads/writes.
- Packing/unpacking energy.
- Decoded-register/cache accesses.
- Cache misses and reconstruction fills.
- Encoder stalls.
- Added pipeline registers.
- Baseline versus proposed execution time.
- Encoder and added-buffer leakage over execution time.

Avoid double-counting costs already included in component energy models.

21. EXPECTED QUALITATIVE ENERGY TREND
======================================================================

Hypothesis from the source discussion:

Energy
  ^
  |\
  | \
  |  \                      Repeated
  |   \                   reconstruction
  |    \                   starts hurting
  |     \                      /
  |      \                    /
  |       \__________________/
  |
  +------------------------------------------------>
      Early                                  Late
                Reconstruction position

Possible explanation:

Moving reconstruction toward computation initially saves:
- Upstream storage traffic.
- NoC traffic.
- Local-buffer traffic.

Extremely late reconstruction may repeatedly regenerate the same weight
for each MAC use.

A decoded-weight latch/cache may shift the best placement later:

Reduced transport --> Reduced SPad --> XOR --> Small full latch --> MAC

IMPORTANT:
The U-shaped curve is a hypothesis, not an expected output to force.
Measured energy can be monotonic or have a different shape depending on
encoder costs, access schedules, packing, fanout, and reuse.

Do not generate synthetic "results" that merely reproduce this sketch.

23. PROPOSED PAPER STORY
======================================================================

The contribution should be broader than:

"We reconstructed ECC bits with XOR gates near the PE."

Proposed framing:

Embedded ECC creates deterministically redundant weight bits. Rather than
propagating those bits through the entire accelerator hierarchy, remove
them after off-chip error correction and regenerate them at a selected
reconstruction boundary.

Moving this boundary closer to computation can reduce storage and
communication costs, but can increase reconstruction multiplicity through
temporal reuse patterns and spatial fanout.

Conceptual DSE:

                   +----------------------+
                   | Reconstruction DSE   |
                   +-----------+----------+
                               |
           +-------------------+-------------------+
           v                   v                   v
    Storage hierarchy    Network fanout     Temporal reuse
           |                   |                   |
           +-------------------+-------------------+
                               |
                               v
                 Best reconstruction boundary
                 under measured constraints

Evaluate generality across:
1. Eyeriss v2 / RS+.
2. Weight stationary.
3. Eyeriss v1 / RS.
4. Output stationary.
5. Input stationary.
6. Simba.

Eyeriss v2 remains the initial and primary implementation target.

25. FIVE MAIN RISKS TO THE STUDY
======================================================================

1. ECC DEPENDENCY GRANULARITY

Determine how many retained bits and weights must be simultaneously
available to reconstruct one group.

Do not assume every weight can be reconstructed independently.


2. PHYSICAL SRAM PACKING

Show that the reduced representation lowers physical accesses, memory
width, or another characterized physical cost.

Do not claim savings merely because unused bit positions contain zeros.


3. EYERISS-V2 CSC INTERACTION

Account for address/count/column metadata.

Do not equate value-bit reduction with total compressed-representation
reduction.

Check whether ECC grouping is compatible with sparse ordering and
zero-skipping.


4. FANOUT VERSUS RECONSTRUCTION MULTIPLICITY

Post-multicast reconstruction can save transport energy while replicating:
- Encoder instances.
- Reconstruction events.
- Area.
- Leakage.
- Potential buffering.

Measure the complete tradeoff.


5. TEMPORAL REUSE VERSUS RECONSTRUCTION MULTIPLICITY

Do not compare only encoder energy per reconstruction.

Also compare:

Encoder energy per useful MAC after amortization.

Count actual reconstructed-result reuse under the execution schedule.

======================================================================
IMPLEMENTATION HANDOFF NOTES
======================================================================

These notes make the implications of the discussion explicit; they are
not claims that an implementation already exists.

Before making architectural changes:

1. Inspect the repository and its existing Timeloop/Accelergy flow.
2. Identify the actual baseline architecture configuration.
3. Inspect the ECC parameters, generator relationships, and weight mapping.
4. Establish reconstruction granularity and dependency availability.
5. Define physically packed reduced representations, including metadata.
6. Implement the initial Eyeriss-v2 variants.
7. Derive reconstruction counts from actual events and reuse schedules.
8. Characterize the corresponding encoder implementation.
9. Combine costs without double-counting.
10. Compare energy, area, and latency against an equivalent baseline.

Do not assume:
- A default Eyeriss-like model is Eyeriss v2.
- Weights reside in a global buffer in every architecture.
- Half the value bits means half the SRAM or NoC energy.
- Every weight can be reconstructed independently.
- A single latch captures all reuse.
- Late reconstruction always wins.
- An added encoder is free in timing, area, leakage, or control.
- The qualitative ratings are experimental results.

The deliverable is a reproducible, configurable evaluation that can
determine the best reconstruction boundary, not an implementation designed
to confirm a predetermined winner.
