PART 1: PROJECT FOUNDATIONS AND ARCHITECTURES — SECTIONS 1–11

Source conversation:
https://chatgpt.com/share/6a9cf72e-979c-83e9-932b-c6193b111007

PURPOSE OF THIS FILE

This file provides project context for an LLM coding assistant implementing
an architectural design-space exploration of ECC-derived weight
reconstruction in AI accelerators.

The primary target is Eyeriss v2. The methodology should also support:
- Eyeriss v1.
- Generic weight-stationary (WS) architectures.
- Generic output-stationary (OS) architectures.
- Generic input-stationary (IS) architectures.
- Simba.

The central idea is to retain only the independent portion of an embedded-ECC
weight representation within selected parts of the on-chip hierarchy, then
reconstruct the omitted dependent bits using XOR encoders before computation.

Example:
- Original embedded weight representation: 8 bits.
- Retained information: approximately 3–4 bits per weight.
- Remaining bits: deterministically reconstructed from ECC relationships.

IMPORTANT:
The per-weight example is illustrative. Actual ECC dependencies may span
multiple weights in a codeword. Part 2, section 15 explains this constraint.
Do not implement an independent per-weight encoder unless the ECC mapping
actually supports it.

The research question is not simply where XOR gates can be inserted. It is
where the reduced representation should stop and the full representation
should resume to minimize total cost while meeting throughput, timing,
capacity, and reliability requirements.

Three central quantities:
1. How many bits can remain omitted up to a candidate boundary?
2. How much temporal reuse occurs after reconstruction?
3. How much spatial fanout occurs before or after reconstruction?

Working framing:
"Reconstruction-boundary optimization across the accelerator
memory/interconnect hierarchy."

STATUS OF THE MATERIAL

- Architecture descriptions summarize the source discussion.
- Proposed placements, ratings, and expected winners are hypotheses.
- No measured energy savings, area results, or timing results are established
  by this document.
- Verify exact architecture configurations against the selected papers,
  repository configurations, and hardware models before implementation.
- Generic WS, OS, and IS describe dataflows; they do not uniquely specify a
  physical architecture.
- The original chat included citation markers. Those opaque chat-local
  markers are omitted here; they are not usable bibliographic references.

Historical motivation from the source discussion:
The original Eyeriss-style 65-nm energy comparison placed a DRAM access at
roughly 200 times MAC energy, a large global-buffer access at approximately
6 times, an inter-PE/array transfer at approximately 2 times, and a small RF
access at approximately 1 time.

These are historical illustrative ratios, not parameters to reuse
uncritically for a new technology or implementation.

1. CORRECT CONCEPTUAL MODEL FOR THE ECC SCHEME
======================================================================

An oversimplified view is:

DRAM --> SRAM --> RF --> PE
          ^
       Encoder?

The more useful model separates the off-chip reliability domain from the
on-chip reduced-representation domain:

                       OFF-CHIP RELIABILITY DOMAIN
+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| DRAM: full embedded-ECC representation                          |
|          |                                                     |
|          v                                                     |
| ECC decode / error correction                                  |
+----------+-----------------------------------------------------+
           |
           | Discard bits that can be deterministically rebuilt.
           v
       Reduced representation
       Example: 4 retained bits instead of 8 full bits
           |
           |    ON-CHIP RECONSTRUCTION DOMAIN
           |
           +-------------- R1
           v
       Global storage
           |
           +-------------- R2
           v
          NoC
           |
           +-------------- R3
           v
       Local weight storage
           |
           +-------------- R4
           v
      Weight register/latch
           |
           +-------------- R5
           v
          MAC

Research question:

How deep into the accelerator can the reduced representation be propagated
before the cost of repeated reconstruction becomes larger than the
additional storage and communication energy saved?

Reliability constraint:

If the complete embedded-ECC representation in DRAM provides error
correction, the reconstructable bits generally must not be discarded
before the DRAM-side ECC check and correction.

Required conceptual sequence:

DRAM full ECC codeword
        |
        v
Read complete protected codeword
        |
        v
ECC syndrome computation / correction
        |
        v
Corrected codeword
        |
        v
Retain independent/systematic portion
        |
        v
Accelerator reduced-representation datapath

Potential benefits:
- Lower memory-controller-to-accelerator bandwidth.
- Lower on-chip SRAM capacity requirements.
- Lower on-chip SRAM access traffic or access count.
- Lower NoC traffic.
- Lower PE weight-storage traffic.

Do not automatically claim reduced DRAM-array read energy. The complete
protected codeword may still need to be read to perform error correction.

Reconstructing at an SRAM input restores full-width data before storage,
so that SRAM does not receive the reduced-storage benefit. Upstream
transport may still benefit if it carried the reduced representation.

2. MOST IMPORTANT PLACEMENT PRINCIPLE
======================================================================

Illustrative assumptions:
- Full weight representation: 8 bits.
- Reduced representation: 4 bits.
- Reconstruction: several XOR operations.

2.1 EARLY RECONSTRUCTION

4-bit SRAM
   |
   | Read 4 bits
   v
Encoder
   |
   | 8 bits
   v
NoC --8 bits--> RF --8 bits--> MAC

Benefits:
- Reduced SRAM storage.
- Potential SRAM access-energy savings.

Limitations:
- No reduced-width NoC traffic after the encoder.
- No reduced-width RF storage or traffic.


2.2 LATE RECONSTRUCTION

4-bit SRAM
   |
   +--4 bits--> NoC --4 bits--> 4-bit RF
                                  |
                                  v
                                Encoder
                                  |
                                  | 8 bits
                                  v
                                 MAC

Potential benefits:
- Reduced SRAM storage.
- Reduced SRAM traffic.
- Reduced NoC traffic.
- Reduced RF storage.
- Reduced RF read bit-volume.

Main risk:
The encoder may execute once for every MAC use of the weight.


2.3 LATE RECONSTRUCTION WITH A RECONSTRUCTED-WEIGHT LATCH/CACHE

4-bit SRAM
   |
   +--4 bits--> NoC --4 bits--> 4-bit weight SPad
                                  |
                                  | Read once
                                  v
                               XOR encoder
                                  |
                                  | 8 bits
                                  v
                         Reconstructed-weight
                            register/latch
                                  |
                                  +--> reuse
                                  +--> reuse
                                  +--> reuse
                                  +--> reuse --> MAC

Goal:
Preserve most benefits of late reconstruction without paying reconstruction
energy for every reuse.

This is particularly promising for weight-stationary architectures and
Simba.

A latch and a cache are different implementations:
- A latch/register can retain the currently active weight.
- A cache can retain multiple reconstructed weights but adds lookup,
  replacement, and storage costs.

The appropriate mechanism depends on the actual weight-access schedule.

3. RATING SYSTEM
======================================================================

The source discussion used architectural promise ratings, not experimental
results.

| Rating | Interpretation |
|--------|----------------|
| 5/5 | Extremely promising reconstruction boundary |
| 4/5 | Strong candidate worth evaluating |
| 3/5 | Useful tradeoff point |
| 2/5 | Usually less attractive than another placement |
| 1/5 | Generally not worthwhile |

All ratings assume the reduced representation is physically exploited:
- Packed weights.
- Fewer accesses.
- Narrower transfers.
- Appropriate memory organization.

Merely leaving unused bits in a full-width word does not establish
proportional energy savings.

4. ENERGY MODEL
======================================================================

For reconstruction placement r, define:

n:
    Full representation width.

k:
    Retained representation width, where k < n.

A_l:
    Number of accesses/transfers at hierarchy level l.

E_l(b):
    Energy of accessing/transferring b bits at hierarchy level l.

N_rec(r):
    Number of reconstructions at placement r.

E_rec:
    Energy per reconstruction.

E_control(r):
    Additional control energy associated with placement r.

Conceptual model:

E(r) =
    SUM over l in partial(r) [A_l * E_l(k)]
  + SUM over l in full(r)    [A_l * E_l(n)]
  + N_rec(r) * E_rec
  + E_control(r)

Boundary interpretation:

Reduced representation | Full representation
                       ^
                  Reconstruction

Moving the boundary toward computation can save more data movement, but
may increase N_rec.

Implementation interpretation:
This is a conceptual accounting model. If packing or remapping changes
physical access counts, use placement-dependent physical counts rather
than assuming A_l remains identical for every variant.

The complete evaluation must also account for relevant leakage, buffering,
packing/unpacking, and timing overheads.

Use a consistent granularity for n, k, and E_rec:
- Per weight, only if reconstruction is independent per weight.
- Per ECC group/codeword, if dependencies span multiple weights.

5. EYERISS V2 — PRIMARY TARGET
======================================================================

5.1 ACTUAL HIERARCHY

Eyeriss v2 uses:
- Row-stationary-plus (RS+) dataflow.
- A hierarchical-mesh network on chip (HM-NoC).
- PE clusters.
- Router clusters.
- Global-buffer clusters.
- Separate networks for different data types.
- Communication modes supporting different reuse patterns.

The source discussion describes published PE-local CSC weight storage as:
- Weight-data scratchpad: 96 x 24 bits.
- Weight-address scratchpad: 16 x 7 bits.
- Capacity corresponding to up to 192 12-bit compressed weight entries.

These implementation-specific quantities should be verified before
hard-coding them into the model.

Important distinction:
The original Eyeriss-v2 GLB should not automatically be modeled as a
conventional large global weight SRAM. The source discussion states that
its GLB SRAM banks serve input activations and partial sums, with dedicated
weight ports feeding the weight network.

5.4 INITIAL EYERISS V2 VARIANTS

EV2-A: RECONSTRUCT AT PE WEIGHT-SPAD INPUT

DRAM/ECC
   |
   v
4 bits ---- HM-NoC ----> Encoder --8 bits--> Weight SPad --> MAC

Expected savings:
- Memory-interface-to-PE traffic.
- Hierarchical-mesh bandwidth.
- NoC switching.

Not reduced by this placement:
- PE weight-SPad capacity.
- PE weight-SPad read width.

Reconstruction frequency:
Approximately once per weight loaded into the local SPad, subject to actual

EV2-B: RECONSTRUCT AT WEIGHT-SPAD OUTPUT

DRAM/ECC
   |
   v
4 bits ---- HM-NoC ----> 4-bit Weight SPad --> Encoder --8 bits--> MAC

Additional potential savings:
- PE weight-memory capacity.
- PE weight-memory write bit-volume.
- PE weight-memory read bit-volume.

Risk:
Reconstruction count can increase substantially.


EV2-C: WEIGHT-SPAD OUTPUT PLUS DECODED-WEIGHT LATCH

4-bit SPad --> XOR encoder --> 8-bit decoded weight register --> MAC
                  ^                         |
                  |                         +--> Reuse across MAC uses
                  |
           Reload/reconstruct when the required weight changes

Leading hypothesis from the source discussion: 5/5.

Potential benefits:
- Reduced HM-NoC traffic.
- Reduced SPad storage.
- Reduced SPad bit traffic.
- Amortized reconstruction.

The full-width register may be small compared with the SPad, but its actual
benefit depends on access order and reuse locality.

        |
        v
 Full stationary-weight latch
        |
        +--> R3b: reconstruct once, reuse full weight     [5/5]
        v

Illustrative example: one weight participates in 100 MACs.

Reconstruction before RF:
- 1 reconstruction.
- 100 full-width RF reads, under this illustrative access model.

Reduced RF plus reconstruction on every read:
- 100 reduced-width RF reads.
- 100 reconstructions.

Reduced RF plus stationary decoded register:
- 1 reduced RF read.
- 1 reconstruction.
- 100 register reuses.

These counts describe alternative access organizations, not a universal
property of all WS hardware. A fair baseline should include any stationary
register and reuse mechanism already present.

8. OUTPUT-STATIONARY
======================================================================

In output-stationary dataflow, partial sums remain local while weights and
activations are supplied to the PE.

Weights ----> PE: partial sum remains here
Inputs  ----> PE

Canonical hierarchy:

DRAM
  |
  v
Weight buffer / global SRAM
  |
  v
NoC / systolic injection
  |
  v
PE
  |
  +--> Streamed weight
  +--> Streamed activation
  +--> Stationary partial-sum RF
  |
  v
Output

Weights are streaming operands in this abstraction.

Reconstructing much earlier than the PE expands the weight representation
while it is still being transported.

Promising path:

4-bit weight ---- NoC ----> PE --> XOR --> MAC

Earlier alternative:

4-bit weight --> XOR --> 8-bit weight ---- NoC ----> PE

The earlier alternative sacrifices reduced-width NoC traffic.

High multicast fanout can change the result:

Option 1:
1 encoder + full-width multicast to 100 PEs.

Option 2:
100 destination encoders + reduced-width multicast.

Evaluate both rather than assuming late reconstruction always wins.

10. SIMBA
======================================================================

Simba provides a multi-level weight-stationary architecture.

Hierarchy:

Package
   |
   v
Chiplet
   |
   v
PE

Communication:
- Network on package (NoP) between chiplets.
- Network on chip (NoC) within a chiplet.

The source discussion describes each PE as containing:
- Distributed weight buffer.
- Input buffer.
- Parallel vector-MAC units.
- Accumulation buffer.
- Post-processing hardware.

Key feature:

are supplied each cycle.

This aligns with reconstruction immediately before stationary weight

11. CROSS-ARCHITECTURE COMPARISON
======================================================================

These rankings are hypotheses.

| Architecture | Early/global reconstruction | After NoC | Local weight-storage output | Local output + decoded reuse register | Likely best candidate |
|--------------|-----------------------------|-----------|-----------------------------|---------------------------------------|-----------------------|
| Eyeriss v2 | 2/5 | 4/5 | 3/5 | 5/5 | SPad output plus decoded microcache/latch |
| Weight stationary | 3/5 | 4/5 | 2/5 for naive repeated encoding | 5/5 | Reduced RF plus decoded stationary register |
| Eyeriss v1 | 3/5 | 4/5 | 3/5 | 5/5 | PE weight SPad/RF output plus reuse latch |
| Output stationary | 3/5 | 5/5 | 4/5 | Less central in the streaming abstraction | Destination-side NoC/PE input |
| Input stationary | 3/5 | 5/5 | 4/5 | Less central in the streaming abstraction | Destination-side NoC/PE input |
| Simba | 2/5 | 4/5 | 5/5 | 5/5 | Distributed weight-buffer output to vector-MAC registers |

General hypothesis:

Architectures with strong local weight reuse favor reconstruction
immediately before a small stationary decoded-weight register.

Architectures with streaming weights favor reconstruction after the
weight-distribution network, subject to fanout, forwarding, and encoder
throughput costs.

Do not hard-code these rankings as the outcome of the DSE.
The implementation must measure the relevant counts and costs.
